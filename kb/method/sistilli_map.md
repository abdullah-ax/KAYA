# Sistilli's startup map

## Concepts worth teaching

- **Fake Steve Jobs.** Great products come from iterating with users, not from your head. ([page](https://map.sistilli.dev/public/startups/concepts/Fake+Steve+Jobs))
- **Choose a problem, not a solution.** Most people think of ideas as solutions, which is backwards. ([page](https://map.sistilli.dev/public/startups/concepts/How+to+choose+an+idea))
- **Tarpit ideas.** Common problems that look easy to solve usually aren't; ask why no one has solved them. The page credits Startup School. ([page](https://map.sistilli.dev/public/startups/concepts/Tarpit+idea))
- **Painkiller vs vitamin.** People pay more for a painkiller than a vitamin. The author says the price ranges come from one YouTube video and may not be accurate, so we use the idea, not the numbers. ([page](https://map.sistilli.dev/public/startups/concepts/Pricing+-+Painkiller+vs+Vitamin))
- **Customer segments are a who-where pair.** If you don't know where to find them, slice the segment smaller. ([page](https://map.sistilli.dev/public/startups/The+Mom+Test/pre-conversations/Customer+Segments))
- **Good questions** (from his Mom Test notes): ask how they do it today and what they've tried, not "would you buy this?". ([page](https://map.sistilli.dev/public/startups/The+Mom+Test/conversations/Questions))
- **First customers** already have the problem, know it, have a budget, and have built their own makeshift fix. ([page](https://map.sistilli.dev/public/startups/The+Mom+Test/post-product/First+customers))

## Where we disagree

Sistilli suggests skipping validation when founder-market fit is strong. We still run a short test with a target set in advance, because the organizers require a Validate gate and because our users are often first-time founders. We say so to the founder plainly.
